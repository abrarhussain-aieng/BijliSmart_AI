# Electricity billing concepts and saving guidelines

## Units
One "unit" on an electricity bill is one kilowatt-hour (kWh): a 1000 W load running for 1 hour.

## Common parts of a bill
- Energy (electricity) charges: units consumed multiplied by the applicable tariff rate.
- Fuel Price Adjustment (FPA) and other adjustments: variable charges notified by the regulator and added to the bill.
- Taxes and duties such as General Sales Tax (GST) and other levies.
- Other charges: fixed charges, meter rent, arrears or late payment surcharge where applicable.
- Total payable amount: the sum payable by the due date.

## Tariffs
Residential tariffs in Pakistan are slab based, so the rate per unit can change with the number of units consumed.
Tariff categories, slab limits and rates are determined by the regulator and government and change over time.
This guide does not contain current rates. Use the official NEPRA and distribution company (DISCO, for example FESCO)
tariff documents placed in the knowledge base to answer questions about rates.

## Reading your own cost per unit
Dividing the total bill amount by the units consumed gives an average cost per unit for that month.
This average includes taxes and adjustments, so it is higher than the base energy rate.

## Practical energy-saving guidelines
- Air conditioning: use a moderate thermostat setting (around 26 C), a timer, clean filters and keep doors and windows closed.
- Use LED lighting and switch lights and fans off in empty rooms.
- Refrigerator: keep door seals in good condition, avoid placing hot food inside, keep space around the unit for ventilation.
- Water heater: use a timer and avoid keeping the heater on all day.
- Iron clothes in batches; run washing machines with full loads.
- Run water pumps only when needed.
- Unplug chargers and devices that stay on standby.
- Shifting flexible loads away from the evening peak can help where time-of-use pricing applies.
